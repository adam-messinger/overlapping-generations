## Calibration this backcast forces

The original efficiency parameterization (η₀=0.35, unsourced; λ=0.25,
guessed) under-predicted history by a steady ~0.6-0.9 pp/yr — a missing
multiplicative residual (backcast 2025 GDP: $117T, 26% under observed).
η₀=0.35 claimed 1990’s efficiency was already near the 0.60 ceiling,
strangling the learning headroom. The exergy-economics literature
measures world second-law efficiency far lower. Recalibration:

- **η(1990) = 0.15** — De Stercke (2014) world exergy efficiency
- **η_max = 0.6** — Cullen & Allwood (2010) practical potential
- **λ = 0.21** — solved so the 1990-anchored backcast reproduces observed 2025 GDP
- **Implied η(2025) = 0.233** — inside Brockway et al. (2018)’s measured ~0.20-0.25, an
  independent check the solver was not fitted to
- **Forward cumulativeWorkHistory = 42** — the 1990 base (30yr) plus observed
  1990-2024 useful work expressed in 2025 units, so the forward run sits
  at the correct point of the same learning curve

Three independent anchors (1990 level, 2025 level, 35-year growth path)
are mutually consistent — the calibration is over-identified, not fitted.
Pinned in `production.test.ts`.

## Demand-side check: the energy-GDP relationship

The demand module ties energy services to GDP (unit income elasticity)
minus autonomous intensity decline. Observed 1990-2025:

- GDP 3.43%/yr, final energy 1.41%/yr → observed intensity
  decline 2.02%/yr; long-run energy-GDP elasticity 0.41
- Model structure at observed GDP growth: final energy ≈ 2.10%/yr
  (1.33%/yr GDP-weighted intensity decline) vs 1.41%/yr observed —
  the model over-predicts energy demand growth at high GDP growth.

At moderate GDP growth (~2%/yr) the two decompositions nearly agree
(model: ~0.7%/yr energy growth; observed-elasticity view: ~0.8%/yr), so
the unit-elasticity-minus-decline structure is adequate for the
forward range but conservative-to-loose at the extremes: it would
overstate energy demand in a fast-growth world (understating intensity
improvements that historically accompanied growth) and understate the
floor in a stagnant one. Worth revisiting only if forward GDP growth
moves outside ~1-3%/yr.

## Caveats

- Observed series are approximate (±5-10%); the pre-calibration
  shortfall was several times that uncertainty.
- Labor uses working-age headcount; education enters via collegeShare →
  organizational efficiency, mirroring the model’s own separation.
- Damages, energy-burden, food stress, and system overheads are zeroed
  on both sides of the comparison (small, roughly constant shares).
- 2020 covid dip is in the observed GDP series; per-decade rows bracket
  it, and the 2020-2025 row is inflated by the rebound.
