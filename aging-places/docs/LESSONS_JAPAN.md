# Japan and Korea: contextual hypotheses

This note supplies qualitative priors for the mechanism model. It is not a validation sample, and
no US coefficient is estimated from the examples below. Earlier versions contained many precise
local price claims without a reproducible dataset; those claims have been removed.

## What the official data establish

Japan's 2023 Housing and Land Survey reports 9.002 million vacant dwellings, or 13.8% of the stock,
both record highs. The same official system publishes municipality-level results for places with
population at least 15,000. This establishes that a very old society can have abundant housing and
large vacancy at the same time; it does not by itself identify which municipal features cause price
resilience.

## Hypotheses carried into the US model

- **Demand can concentrate while national household growth slows.** Major labor markets and
  regional service centers may gain share even if the country grows little.
- **Vacancy and amenity are different concepts.** Seasonal/coastal characteristics do not protect
  value without purchasers, access, income, or constrained supply.
- **Supply mediates price response.** Strong demand can appear as construction rather than price
  appreciation; surplus stock can intensify weak-demand outcomes.
- **A one-cohort settlement can age synchronously.** This motivates the 45–64 share and replacement
  ratio, but does not prove that US retirement communities will repeat a specific Japanese path.
- **Institutional stock is not permanent throughput.** Enrollment, budgets, and branch viability
  can shrink. The current US model does not endogenize those changes.
- **Access is conditional.** Proximity to a strong market can support spillover, but a transport link
  alone does not create local demand.

## What is not transferred mechanically

Japan and Korea differ from the United States in immigration, tenure, mortgage finance, land-use
law, municipal geography, construction practice, and the speed of aging. Named international
examples should be used to propose sensitivity tests, not to assert a US locality outcome. In
particular, the US simulation has no local fertility model, no rail-network model, and no endogenous
institutional decline.
