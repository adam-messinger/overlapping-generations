# Italy, Spain, and Germany: contextual hypotheses

This note treats European housing experience as a source of mechanisms to test, not a set of
coefficient estimates. Earlier versions mixed non-comparable city series and precise unsourced
claims; those have been removed. Eurostat's harmonized House Price Index is the preferred source
for national comparisons, but the project does not ingest it into the US model.

## Comparative questions

Italy, Spain, and Germany provide useful contrasts because aging occurred alongside different
patterns of immigration, urban systems, supply, and regional economic performance. Those contrasts
motivate questions rather than clean causal controls:

- Does external migration raise the national housing-demand floor while concentrating demand in
  particular gateways?
- Do polycentric labor markets distribute resilience more broadly than a dominant-capital system?
- When do university, government, or medical functions generate continuing inflow rather than
  merely mark an institution's presence?
- Can demolition or abandonment stabilize a surplus market without restoring growth?
- Does amenity demand depend on buyers whose income originates outside the municipality?

## Model mappings

- Foreign-born share is a gateway proxy; international immigration is a separate open flow.
- Population access and regional dominance represent metropolitan connection and service-hub
  geometry.
- Institutional employment and enrollment are current capacity proxies.
- Seasonal housing represents revealed external/amenity demand.
- New construction, density, and distress vacancy proxy supply response and surplus.

These mappings are incomplete. The model does not observe foreign purchases, tourism receipts,
transport networks, demolition programs, local planning law, firm entry, or fiscal transfers.

## Evidence standard

Use harmonized official sources for national comparisons and clearly identified local transaction
or appraisal datasets for city claims. Do not combine nominal asking prices, transaction indices,
land appraisals, and rents as if they were one outcome. Relevant official sources are collected in
[REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md).
