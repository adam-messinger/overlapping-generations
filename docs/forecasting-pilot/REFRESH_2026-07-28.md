Those are server-only EIA quantities, not the question's full-site
data-center numerator. The existing named semantic crosswalk remains
necessary.

The
[Census private-construction workbook](https://www.census.gov/construction/c30/xlsx/privsatime.xlsx)
reports data-center construction at a `59.307` billion dollar seasonally
adjusted annual rate in May 2026, versus `48.210` billion a year earlier and
`9.326` billion in May 2021. That is `23.0%` year-over-year growth and a
`44.8%` five-year compound annual growth rate. Construction spending is a
realization signal, not energized capacity, utilization, or electricity
consumption.

The refresh therefore keeps the EIA family as the low structural branch but
reduces its explicit analyst weight from `25%` to `20%`. The seeded mixture
moves to:

- `19.2015%` below 10%;
- `26.2650%` from 10% to below 15%;
- `27.8130%` from 15% to below 20%; and
- `26.7205%` at 20% or more.

Its median is `15.75%`, with a `7.84%–24.92%` 10th–90th percentile interval.
The human forecast stays `18 / 25 / 29 / 28%`: the new evidence reweights
already-represented structural families but does not escape the broader human
uncertainty envelope.

**News/conventional-wisdom comparison:** the data agree strongly with the
headline story that AI/data-center building is exceptionally rapid. The
forecast qualifies the common leap from current construction growth to a
literal 20%-plus 2035 electricity share: spending, energized load, full-site
power, utilization, and the national denominator are different quantities.
Twenty percent remains a serious possibility, not the base case.

## Hormuz

The captured [Straits.live status](https://straits.live/status) reports the
latest mirrored PortWatch day, 23 July, at `10` total calls versus an `88`
pre-crisis baseline (`11.4%`). Eight of the nine named major carriers are
still marked rerouting and one limited; none is marked back in regular
service. Neither the traffic nor carrier-return trigger fired.

The
[liquid Polymarket comparator](https://polymarket.com/event/strait-of-hormuz-traffic-returns-to-normal-by-december-31)
was `56.5%` Yes on about `$6.19 million` volume. Its target is easier: any
seven-day moving average of 60 through 31 December, rather than the
workbench's fixed 1–21 December mean of at least 62. The update transferred
one third of the comparator's change in log odds from its 23 July `49.5%`
checkpoint:

```text
logit(Pnew) = logit(0.39)
            + (1/3) * [logit(0.565) - logit(0.495)]
Pnew = 0.4125 -> sealed at 0.41
```

The physical scenario simulation remains non-identifying: it still explains
what follows conditional reopening or prolonged closure, but provides no
political path weights and no calibrated oil-volume-to-all-vessel-count
crosswalk.

**News/conventional-wisdom comparison:** the update agrees modestly with
renewed-talks optimism and the market's upward move. It still disagrees with a
simple “deal headlines mean normalization” reading: physical traffic and
commercial carrier behavior remain near the closure regime, so No is still
favored `59%` to `41%`.
